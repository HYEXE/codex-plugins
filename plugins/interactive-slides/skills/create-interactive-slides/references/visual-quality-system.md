# Commercial presentation visual-quality system

Use commercial presentation products as a benchmark for finish, not as templates to copy. Derive reusable principles, then create an original art direction appropriate to the user's content and brand.

## Art direction before layout

Define one sentence for each item before building slides:

- editorial premise: what the deck should feel like
- typography: display face, reading face, numeral treatment
- palette: background, foreground, accent and semantic colors
- image treatment: crop, mask, grading, caption and credit style
- geometry: grid, margins, corner language, line weight and icon style
- motion language: one transition family and one emphasis pattern

Prefer a supplied brand system. Otherwise select one coherent direction such as editorial report, cinematic keynote, restrained corporate, data publication or workshop canvas. Do not mix directions slide by slide.

## Slide-family system

Create a small master family instead of repeating one universal card layout:

- cover and section divider
- statement or quote
- evidence with source
- image-led narrative
- comparison or decision
- process, timeline or diagram
- interactive scene
- closing takeaway

Keep tokens consistent across the family, but vary composition, scale and whitespace to create rhythm. Important slides should have a distinct silhouette when viewed as thumbnails.

## Anti-slop rules

Avoid these default AI-generation signatures unless the content specifically requires them:

- a grid of three or four rounded cards on most slides
- decorative gradients, glows, blobs or glass panels without narrative purpose
- excessive pills, badges and tiny uppercase labels
- equal visual weight for every element
- generic stock icons mixed with emoji or unrelated illustration styles
- headings that wrap awkwardly because the copy was not edited
- invented metrics, decorative charts or fake product UI
- identical reveal animation on every object
- body text that behaves like a document rather than a speaking cue
- placing controls inside the slide when they belong in presentation chrome

Every decorative element must support hierarchy, grouping, direction, evidence or brand recognition. Remove it otherwise.

## Professional composition rules

- Use a deliberate grid and optical alignment, including a consistent safe area for projectors.
- Give each slide one dominant idea and one dominant visual anchor.
- Edit copy to fit; do not solve excess text only by shrinking type.
- Use contrast through scale, weight, position and whitespace before adding color.
- Keep chart labels readable and show only data needed for the spoken claim.
- Use high-resolution, source-appropriate imagery with consistent crops and credits.
- Use one icon family with a consistent stroke, optical size and bounding box.
- Keep interactive controls visually secondary to the slide message.
- Render panel controls as icon buttons with accessible names and tooltips.

## Benchmark pass

Review the entire deck as a commercial template author would:

1. Thumbnail pass: hierarchy and rhythm are legible without reading body text.
2. Distance pass: titles and critical numbers remain readable on a projector.
3. Consistency pass: typography, color, grid, image treatment and iconography stay coherent.
4. Variety pass: adjacent slides do not repeat the same composition without reason.
5. Evidence pass: claims, simulations and sources are visibly distinguishable.
6. Interaction pass: controls look intentional, have clear affordances and do not resemble slide content.
7. Polish pass: no orphan words, accidental wrapping, overflow, placeholder copy or uncredited assets.
